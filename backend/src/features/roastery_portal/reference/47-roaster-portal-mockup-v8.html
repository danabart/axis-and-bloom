FROM: AXIS & BLOOM — TO: OUR ROASTERS
One coffee per page, the same six movements as the paper sheet, in the same order, with the same words. A roaster who met the sheet at the cupping table recognizes the form; a roaster who didn't needs no training.
The roaster is greeted by name and their ten coffees are already on the page, with a check on the ones they have finished and a dashed chip for one we missed. No time estimate: the lede says what the answers are for instead, the Bloom Dial and the imagery each coffee will get. Fields we prefilled carry a small FROM YOUR SITE tag so they know what to correct rather than retype.
The flavor-wheel category became the Bloom Dial question: six family cards, each with a slot for its small dial image, a number, the name, and the three words. Experimental carries its own warning so it is not chosen for merely interesting coffees. The roaster proposes; we confirm. The "relative to your lineup" rule is stated twice, in the lede and above the scales, because it is the one thing roasters get wrong.
Fields are left aligned with hairline underlines, the site's own input style. Chips replace dropdowns so every option is visible at once; that is faster on a phone and shows the roaster the shape of our vocabulary.
Type: Genova Medium for the headline and section titles, Thin for lede and help, Regular for labels and chips, Black for the button. Gotham ampersands and question marks. Palette only, no pure white. (This draft renders in a stand-in sans until the Genova files arrive.)
For Dana: every chip and dot is a radio or checkbox under the hood, so this maps one to one onto the intake spreadsheet columns. Autosave as draft, "Save and add another" loops back to 01 with the roaster's name and sizes remembered.
